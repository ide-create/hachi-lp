Japanese Household Rules
Your Housekeeper Can Handle for You

November 5, 2026 | Operated by Sanesu Service Co., Ltd. (30 years employing foreign workers in Japan)

Living in Japan means a handful of household habits that are easy to get wrong at first. A housekeeper who knows your home and the local rules can quietly take much of this off your plate.

Trash sorting and collection days

Japanese cities ask residents to separate waste into categories, and collection days differ by category and by area. Rules vary from one municipality, and sometimes one building, to the next, so always check the guide from your own ward office or building manager. A regular housekeeper can learn your categories and your collection calendar once, then prepare the bags correctly each week.

Shoes, genkan and slippers

Shoes come off at the entrance (genkan). Many homes also keep separate slippers for the toilet and bathroom area. Your housekeeper should follow the same habits, and it helps to tell them which pairs are for which room.

Keeping laundry the way you like it

Households differ in how they sort, dry and fold clothes, and where each item is stored. This is the kind of detail that is tiresome to explain every week to someone new. At Hachi Life it goes into your Family Book once, and the same housekeeper follows it every visit.

Small things that matter

  • Which sponges and cloths are used for which area, such as separate cloths for the toilet.
  • Which items must not be moved or thrown away.
  • How you want things put back after cleaning.

Write it down once

The simplest approach is a single written guide for your home. Hachi Life's Family Book records your rules in Japanese, and our app translates them into English for the housekeeper. After each visit you receive a report of what was done.

A note on scope: Hachi Life covers cleaning, folding and putting away laundry, and shopping errands. Ironing, cooking, childcare and nursing care are not included.
